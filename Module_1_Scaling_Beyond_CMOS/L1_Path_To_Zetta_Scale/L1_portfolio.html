02 — Moore's Law in Practice

50 years of microprocessor
trend data

Transistor counts continue their exponential trajectory (orange). Clock frequency plateaued ~2005 (green). Typical power saturated at 100–125W (red). Performance gains now come from logical core count (black) rather than raw speed.
📸 Slide — 50 Years Microprocessor Trend Data (annotated) ↳ replace with: Screenshot_2026-05-28_at_9_18_12_am.png
IMAGE SLOT 3 images/slide-50years-annotated.png
The same chart annotated with the 2007 iPhone inflection point (blue dashed line) and the current shift toward datacenter-scale computing. Moore's Law is expected to continue — but via new architectural approaches, not just transistor shrinking.

Exponential Transistor Growth

Transistor counts continue an exponential trajectory with no sign of flattening — from thousands in the 1970s to tens of billions today.

Clock Speed Plateau (~2005)

CPU frequencies stagnated between 1–5 GHz due to thermal limits. Chips cannot be cooled fast enough to sustain further clock increases.

Power Wall at ~100–125W

Typical chip power saturated here. The iPhone's 2007 launch accelerated mobile-first design where passive cooling demands extreme efficiency.

Architectural Scaling via Cores

From dual-core mid-2000s to 50–100 cores today. Parallelism became the primary performance vector once clock scaling stopped.

Single-Thread Performance

Still improving (blue curve), but at a slower rate — driven by IPC improvements, cache hierarchy, and microarchitectural cleverness rather than raw frequency.

Expect Moore's Law to Continue

Not in the traditional sense, but through new vectors: 3D stacking, novel transistor geometries (FinFET, GAA), advanced packaging, and chiplet architectures.

03 — Market Inflection

The Rise of Mobile
redefined technology

📸 Slide — Rise of Mobile ↳ replace with: Screenshot_2026-05-28_at_9_19_39_am.png
IMAGE SLOT 4 images/slide-rise-of-mobile.png
Smartphone units shipped crossed PC units shipped around 2010 — three years after the first iPhone launched in 2007. By the mid-2010s, 1.4 billion smartphones were shipped annually vs ~289 million PCs. This crossover forced the entire semiconductor industry to prioritise power efficiency above raw performance, directly causing frequency stagnation and the power wall seen in the microprocessor trend data.

iPhone Released

Apple's 2007 launch triggered a seismic shift. Mobile devices have no active cooling, making power dissipation the dominant design constraint overnight.

Mobile Crosses PC Sales

Smartphone shipments overtook PC shipments globally. The mobile market now dominated technology definition, not desktop computing.

Frequency Suppressed by Mobile

Had mobile not dominated, chip frequencies might have pushed past 100W toward 300W+ envelopes. Instead, industry pivoted to performance-per-watt optimisation.
